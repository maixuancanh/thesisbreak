# Reason review and post-result learning

Approved implementation: extend the existing authenticated research turn rather than create a second chatbot. Every turn reads the exact saved pick version, independent evidence and current approved Walrus lessons. A structured reason review separates supported, contradicted and unresolved claims with supplied evidence references. Price movement and background articles cannot alone establish support or contradiction. No saved reason means no personal reason assessment.

After verified final binary settlement, show a retrospective tied to the exact saved version and timestamp. Compare outcome separately from reasoning quality; entries saved after settlement are not scored. Current evidence is not proof of what was available before the pick. The model can suggest a bounded future-facing lesson with citations; it cannot approve or store it. Reuse the existing lesson review/consent/outbox flow, retaining source run provenance. Existing proposal review remains the only way to apply pick changes.

Validation: negative citation/classification/version/finality/time-order tests, run status schema roundtrip, account isolation, model strict-schema acceptance, browser review controls and normal type/lint/build. No model inference can create settlement facts, source permissions or Walrus approval. Review historical results through This week or the original market conversation; unsupported providers remain without a retrospective.

## Use

In the Thesis panel, **Review my reason** fills a request to assess the saved reason. **Learn from this result** fills a post-result review request. Send it when ready. A saved pick with a reason can show up to four claims under **Does your reason still hold?**, labelled supported, contradicted or unclear. Direct event evidence is required for the first two classifications; price-only and background-only assertions are rejected. These labels are model interpretations with citations, not deterministic truth or prediction recommendations.

**Result & reasoning** reads a verified final public Kalshi binary settlement, the exact owned saved version and its recorded timestamp. The match/not-match/not-scored calculation is performed by code. The model cannot supply or replace it. Retrospectives require the settlement citation and do not treat newly retrieved reports as proof of knowledge available at the time of the pick. Tatum/Polymarket settlement support remains separate.

A suggested lesson stays in the conversation until **Review lesson draft** opens the existing editable lesson form. Category, competition, review and explicit Walrus consent are still required. Only a completed run belonging to the same account can be the lesson's origin. The transaction creates a versioned, consented outbox event; the remote worker must still obtain exact readback before the lesson is shown as verified. Merely generating or opening a draft creates no outbox entry. Pick changes continue through their existing proposal/confirm path.

## Acceptance evidence, 26 October

`pnpm check:reasoning --local-fixture` creates or reuses one labelled local journal scenario in the existing acceptance account, reads actual public source and settlement data, recalls its existing approved Walrus preference and invokes the configured model. It does not write new Walrus memories. The record is intentionally saved after settlement, so its comparison must be not_scored rather than a successful forecast. Private result: `.local/reasoning-loop-proof.json`; fixture receipt: `.local/reasoning-loop-fixture.json`.

The live model produced a cited contradiction of the labelled Cameron Carr starting assumption, a retrospective explicitly identifying the after-settlement timestamp, one cautious lesson draft and one used approved memory. This is integration evidence, not prediction accuracy, real-user adoption or a quality benchmark. Unit tests cover price-only rejection, unknown citations, stale versions, missing finality, late-entry scoring and preservation through status SSE. PostgreSQL tests show a draft produces no outbox, foreign/running-run origins and missing consent are rejected, and a reviewed completed-run lesson is idempotently queued with provenance.

Browser control was disabled during the initial V14 pass. The subsequent V15 pass checked the Google-signed-in flow: a saved final-result review and its cited assessment survive a local service restart; Review lesson draft pre-fills the editable rule/category/competition form; Review preference starts with remote consent unchecked and Approve and remember disabled. Checking consent enables approval; returning to edit resets consent. No lesson was saved during this acceptance check and no new remote memory was created.

Browser inspection also found that the two scope inputs forced the narrow chat panel to scroll horizontally. The grid now allows both columns and their inputs to shrink.
